Fantasy,

We are not looking for a studio to make seven products look related.

We are looking for a partner to help author a language that people learn by using—and that can learn, carefully, from being used.

Across Lakin’s seven instruments, the controls carry meaning. Aim, boundary, standing, return, permission, admission, openness and passage are not decoration. A person should come to know these distinctions in one instrument and carry that fluency into the next, almost as naturally as moving between games that share a controller.

That kind of coherence cannot be delivered as a component library and left behind. It has to survive different products, revisions, designers and, eventually, different custodians. The people who author it must operate it themselves. Documentation can preserve a decision; it cannot substitute for lived fluency.

We think the first real place to build this language is œdit.

The proposed cycle is simple to describe and difficult to do well:

> shared meanings → working instrument → human use → structured returns → design review → deliberately revised grammar

Fantasy would hold design authority for the human form and behaviour of that instrument language. Lakin would remain accountable for what each instrument means, what it is permitted to do, and where its boundaries stand. Pilot returns would inform the work, but they would not automatically become the work. What changes would change only after review and explicit admission.

That distinction matters. Reality should be able to speak through returns without being allowed to rewrite the grammar by accident.

The engagement would begin locally—with œdit, its pilots and the people designing it—but it would be built for inheritance. What survives use and review becomes the next version of a language the other instruments can adopt. The opportunity is therefore not merely to make one interface humane. It is to establish how a family of consequential instruments can remain learnable, coherent and accountable over time.

Some important questions should remain open at the start. The public surface currently speaks of aim, reality and receipt, while the deeper system names a broader family of primitives. We should not hide that mismatch with copy. We should resolve it through the work. We also do not yet know where intended friction becomes obstruction, where consistency should yield to a local instrument, or what users will actually understand. Those are not defects in the invitation. They are part of the assignment.

If this is work you want to enter, the next step is not a seven-product redesign. It is to scope one complete learning cycle around œdit:

- agree the first semantic vocabulary and the responsibilities on each side;
- give it exact human form in the instrument;
- use it ourselves and place it in front of the pilot;
- establish how returns are captured without turning reaction into authority;
- review what transferred, what failed and what remained ambiguous;
- admit a revised grammar with its rationale, exceptions and lineage; and
- determine what the other six instruments may now inherit.

We are asking whether Fantasy wants to design from inside that loop with us—not as a supplier of a finished skin, but as the long-term steward of how this language meets a human being.

If the answer is yes, let us define the first cycle together.

Lakin
