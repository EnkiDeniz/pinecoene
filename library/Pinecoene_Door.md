## `/`  The walk

### Beat 0 — the object

Full dark. The fold already turning. Genesis or This Chat — a real one, not a diagram.

No badge. No “not a fold.” No candidate. No caption that explains.

One word, small, top left: `Pinecœne`

One line under the object, after they have moved it or after two seconds:

`This is a pinecone.`

Hint, quieter:

`Turn it.`

If they do nothing, it keeps turning. The page does not start talking until they have seen it as a thing.

---

### Beat 1 — ideas are scary

Only after the object has been on screen.

Ideas are scary.

They come into this world ugly and messy.

They frighten people because they threaten what is known.

They are the natural-born enemy of the way things are.

The object is still there. Smaller now, or to the side. It does not leave.

---

### Beat 2 — care

Yes. Messy. Fragile.

Under the proper care, they become something beautiful.

---

### Beat 3 — another world

It takes courage to talk about a new idea.

Especially when it feels out of this world.

Some ideas are.

You cannot understand them from the everyday room.

You have to come a little way into the world they came from.

Not because they are complicated.

Because they are strange.

We were not brought up to accommodate them.

---

### Beat 4 — a shape that can travel

This is an idea like that, trying to arrive here.

A thought may need a shape to travel.

Something you can hold.

Something you can offer.

Something that stays open if the work is still living, and stays thin if the work was thin.

That object you turned is one of those shapes.

---

### Beat 5 — the invitation

We may have found a way to do this.

There may even be science under it.

What you are looking at is still messy. It is our idea, being cared into a thing people might want to share.

That care is the whole project.

**Come make a pinecone.**

Bring a messy idea. Watch one become. Help this one become beautiful enough to share.

Buttons:

```
Watch one become
See the works
Bring an idea
```

`Watch one become` plays the object’s own record: a sentence, a hit, a change, a door left open.

`See the works` goes to `/works`.

`Bring an idea` goes to `/join`. It does not pretend the compiler already eats a stranger’s whole life. It is the invitation, stated as what is actually possible today.

---

That is the entire homepage. Five beats after the object. No science. No taxonomy. No floor, middle, ceiling.

## `/join`

Eyebrow: Join

Title: Come make a pinecone.

A messy idea does not become shareable by being explained harder. It becomes shareable by being cared into a form.

What you can do now:

1. Watch a work become, so you know what care looks like.
2. Fork a sketch and operate the instrument in your browser. Your fork cannot pretend to be the original.
3. Send a first ugly sentence — one claim you have not cleaned up. We will not fold a person behind their back. We will only work with what you offer.

What you cannot do yet:

Carry an entire private life into the instrument and receive a finished pinecone by morning. That door is not open.

Buttons:

```
Watch one become
Fork a sketch
Offer a first sentence
```

The third button can be a small form. One field. No ontology questionnaire.

## `/more`  and the latches

A list. Short descriptions. The visitor chooses depth.

### Science

Thought already has geometry in the everyday literature: near and far, centers, paths, two maps that can meet. Shepard. Rosch. Gärdenfors. Lakatos.

What is ours: the path of an inquiry, not the region of a concept, made into an object.

If a thin record still looks finished, the object is lying.

Full probes and names live here, not on the door.

### Art

A form that has to show receipts. Beauty only if it can be unflattering. The pinecone can be kept, turned, shared, returned to — like a record you love. It cannot be posed for.

### How it’s made

A record of what happened.  
A reading you can argue with.  
A form compiled from those two, and only those two.  
Living work stays open.  
A copy with no record goes dark.

The long papers sit under this latch.

### What’s next

A thin fold that looks thin.  
A way for someone to bring more than one sentence.  
A way for two pinecones to meet without one rewriting the other.

Those last two are wishes. They are not shipping.

---

## What this door refuses

To speak before the object.
To make the visitor earn the pinecone by reading a constitution.
To call participation “Use it” before they can bring work.
To put science, vision, art, and technology on the first walk as equal monuments.
To workshop “ideas are scary” into a slogan.

## Test

A first visitor can answer, without scrolling a treatise:

What is that thing?  
Why would anyone need one?  
Can I help make one?

If they cannot, the object is not first, or the invitation is not real.
