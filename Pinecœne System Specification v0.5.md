## 0 · Revision decision

Version 0.5 preserves the relational architecture introduced in v0.4 and makes the first product experience and implementation boundary explicit.

It adds:

- **The Offered Object** — the independent first-sight experience thesis;
- **Encounter Overture** — a recipient-first opening sequence with no account wall;
- **Inspectable Causality** — every expressive feature can disclose why it exists without leaking protected material;
- **Progressive Vocabulary** — human language first, canonical terms revealed through use;
- **Disclosure Compilation** — protected fields are removed before recipient rendering rather than hidden in the interface;
- **Admitted Semantic Digest** — rejected and unadmitted candidates cannot perturb canonical topology;
- **Hashable Payloads** — explicit non-self-referential integrity inputs;
- **Shared-Material Consent** — a declaration layer for records involving other living people;
- **Origin and Portability Law** — one canonical web origin, honest offline guarantees, and honest revocation limits;
- **Production Technical Baseline** — concrete application, player, renderer, storage, packaging, and test choices;
- **Experience and Performance Budgets** — measurable thresholds for wonder that remains usable.

The visual thesis in this revision is a strong default candidate. It may be refined after concept review, but no concept may weaken provenance, disclosure, accessibility, immutability, or owner authority.

> **Pinecœne is a medium through which intelligence in action can acquire a portable body, travel with provenance and boundaries intact, and become generative in an encounter with another intelligence.**

This revision does not create a third profile.

The system still has two profiles:

1. **Fold Profile** — produces persistent owner-held Pinecœnes.
2. **7 Presence Profile** — produces session-scoped face, voice, and accountable assembly telemetry.

Constellation, Address, Animation, Offering, Encounter, Return, and Successor are layers and lifecycle stages around the Fold Profile.

> **One grammar. Two profiles. One relational lifecycle.**

## 1 · Canonical product model

### 1.1 Pinecœne

A **Pinecœne** is an owner-held, versioned, executable conformation compiled from:

- an owner-authorized record;
- a declared reading of that record;
- explicit standing and permissions;
- owner-recognized formative lineage;
- and an owner admission event.

It may be rendered through space, sound, text, story, touch, or another conformant medium.

It is not:

- a direct picture of a mind;
- a personality portrait;
- a psychometric profile;
- a contribution score;
- a decorative data visualization;
- a raw transcript;
- a chain-of-thought display;
- a claim that one true geometry was hidden inside the source.

### 1.2 The larger act

A Pinecœne is the portable object inside a larger act of accountable passage.

The canonical lifecycle is:

```text
RECORD
  → READING
  → RECOGNITION / CONSTELLATION
  → FOLD
  → ANIMATION / EXPRESSION
  → ADDRESS
  → OFFERING
  → ENCOUNTER
  → RETURN
  → SUCCESSOR
```

The lifecycle may stop at any stage.

A Fold may remain private.  
An Address may remain latent.  
An Offering may be declined.  
A Return may remain OPEN.  
A successor may never be admitted.

No stage is compulsory merely because a previous stage occurred.

### 1.3 Three human acts after compilation

The compiler produces a lawful body. The owner then performs three distinct acts:

#### Recognition

The owner confirms which Muses and formative relations mattered.

#### Animation

The owner chooses how the body should live, sound, and feel without changing semantic topology.

#### Address

The owner chooses where the form is pointed and what may open there.

These acts are not machine authorization.

The system may propose candidates. Only the owner may recognize, animate, address, and offer the Fold as theirs.

### 1.4 Experience thesis — The Offered Object

The first experience of Pinecœne is not a dashboard, an ontology lesson, or a visualization demo.

It is the encounter with a quiet, folded object that was intentionally offered by one intelligence to another.

At first sight the object should feel:

- intimate rather than spectacular;
- materially credible rather than graphically decorative;
- composed rather than ambiently busy;
- strange enough to invite attention;
- calm enough to carry trust;
- inspectable enough to deserve belief.

The preferred visual world is a folded reliquary under raking light: paper, mineral, glass, metal, shadow, seam, and aperture. The experience may feel quietly impossible, but it must never feel causally arbitrary.

Pinecœne is not represented by:

- a generic glowing orb;
- a particle nebula;
- a cybernetic heads-up display;
- a literal pinecone;
- decorative artificial-intelligence theater;
- permanent breathing, orbiting, or idle motion that implies life without an event.

The first expressive promise is:

> **This form exists because something happened, someone chose what it meant, and someone chose to offer it to you.**

The product introduces canonical vocabulary progressively. A recipient may first encounter only:

- Pinecœne;
- Muse;
- Locket;
- Return.

Core, Bag, Quarta, Trail, Şekli, Standing, and the complete compiler grammar become available through inspection and Studio use. The system does not require a recipient to learn the ontology before receiving value.

## 2 · Profiles and surfaces

### 2.1 Fold Profile

The Fold Profile produces a persistent Pinecœne.

Eligible subjects include:

- a completed human Œdit;
- a human-authored reading of a source;
- a document or decision;
- a project or product history;
- a Git repository and its admitted development record;
- a relationship or collaboration record with declared seats;
- a personal reflection;
- a work composed for another person.

Canonical Fold objects:

- Trail;
- Şekli;
- Glyph;
- Locket;
- Constellation;
- Replay;
- Whistle;
- Recital;
- Passport.

### 2.2 7 Presence Profile

7 Presence renders observable runtime state as a compact audiovisual identity.

It does not produce:

- a Pinecœne owned by 7;
- a human Fold;
- a personal Muse Constellation;
- a Locket belonging to 7;
- hidden chain-of-thought;
- an automatically persistent memory.

### 2.3 Product surfaces

The implementation should expose five primary surfaces.

#### Fold Studio

Imports and reads the record, preserves source/read separation, supports human encounter, and compiles the candidate Fold.

#### Constellation Pass

Lets the owner review candidate Muses, acknowledge formative lineage, link prior artifacts, control identity and visibility, and reject machine proposals.

#### Offering Canvas

Lets the owner animate the Fold, choose Address, choose resolution, preview the recipient experience, and create an Offering.

#### Witness Player

Lets a recipient encounter the Offering at the granted resolution, without mutating the Fold.

#### 7 Presence

Makes active session state socially legible and performs the handoff between open session and persistent owner Fold.

- diff;

- repair;
- collision;
- direct address;
- delegation;
- evaluation;
- withheld advancement;
- formative resonance.

### 3.3 Standing

What status the owner and instrument currently assign:

- observed;
- candidate;
- contested;
- acknowledged;
- admitted;
- rejected;
- OPEN;
- sealed.

### 3.4 Receipt provenance

Why a claim or relation may stand.

### 3.5 Assembly provenance

Which event, transition, or rule produced a feature of the Pinecœne.

### 3.6 Formative lineage

What entered the maker’s field and altered, accompanied, or reoriented the becoming.

### 3.7 Expression

How the owner chooses to perform the compiled form.

### 3.8 Address

Where the owner points the Offering.

### 3.9 Offering

The bounded act of presenting one exact Fold version through one exact Expression Profile, Address, and disclosure resolution.

### 3.10 Encounter

The recipient’s permitted experience of the Offering.

### 3.11 Return

A new response arriving from the recipient or world.

### 3.12 Successor

A new Pinecœne version produced from admitted subsequent record events.

A successor never mutates its predecessor.

## 4 · Shared material grammar

The material grammar remains:

- **Solid**
- **Particulate**
- **Edge**

The substances may be interpreted across visual, sonic, textual, and physical renderers. Their semantic distinction must survive.

### 4.1 Solid

Committed standing.

In a Fold, Solid may represent:

- admitted aim;
- admitted operation;
- standing proposition;
- owner seal;
- admitted Quarta.

In 7 Presence, Solid remains ghostlike and provisional.

### 4.2 Particulate

Plural external presences.

Particulate contains two explicitly distinct semantic classes:

#### Return particulate

What reality answered:

- receipt;
- objection;
- test;
- witness;
- contradiction;
- confirmation;
- missing coverage;
- recipient return.

#### Muse particulate

What the owner recognizes as having entered or accompanied the becoming:

- person;
- living being;
- work;
- place;
- memory;
- music;
- image;
- object;
- idea;
- event;
- prior Pinecœne.

Return and Muse particulate must not be visually or sonically conflated.

A Muse cannot strengthen standing merely by proximity.

### 4.3 Edge

Boundary and relation.

Edge may represent:

- source boundary;
- Bag;
- scope;
- version;
- exclusion;
- permission;
- Locket state;
- relation between events;
- relation between a Muse and the Fold;
- relation between Muses;
- Address vector;
- operative boundary;
- successor relation.

### 4.4 Quarta

A fourth-point relation that creates a new dimensional possibility.

Quarta is not:

- a label for every insight;
- a visual reward;
- a fourth list item;
- a brightness increase;
- a generic AI “aha.”

Only owner-admitted Quarta may acquire committed volume.

### 4.5 Openness

OPEN must remain perceivable.

- no coverage stays sparse;
- unresolved relation stays unclosed;
- withheld advancement stays withheld;
- an open record never receives a false cadence;
- an unanswered Address remains unanswered;
- a declined Offering is not rendered as rejection of the owner.

### 4.6 Charge

Charge is computed from active receipt binding and freshness.

Charge may affect luminance, resonance, vitality, or another renderer-specific parameter.

Charge may not be manually increased by the Expression Profile.

## 6 · Muse and Constellation specification

### 6.1 Muse definition

A **Muse** is an owner-recognized formative presence that entered, accompanied, resonated with, or reoriented the becoming of a Fold.

A Muse is not automatically:

- evidence;
- causal proof;
- authorization;
- attribution;
- endorsement;
- co-authorship;
- a recipient.

### 6.2 Muse kinds

Version 0.5 supports:

```text
person
living_being
place
work
memory
music
image
object
idea
event
prior_pinecoene
other
```

### 6.3 Muse standing and facts

Muse disposition is a single owner-governed state:

```text
candidate
acknowledged
rejected
```

#### Candidate

Machine- or human-proposed but not owner-recognized.

#### Acknowledged

The owner confirms formative relevance.

#### Rejected

The owner rejects the proposed relation.

Mention and linkage are orthogonal facts, not higher standing:

- `mentionedInRecord` says the Muse appears explicitly in the authorized record;
- `linkedArtifactRef` connects the Muse to an inspectable source or artifact;
- `linkedPinecoeneId` connects the Muse to a separate Pinecœne;
- none of these facts acknowledges the Muse on the owner’s behalf.

### 6.4 Muse relations

Version 0.5 supports:

```text
thought_of
remembered
reminded_by
resonated_with
learned_from
grew_from
grew_among
accompanied_by
reoriented_by
resembled
returned_to
connected_through
inspired_by
witnessed_by
```

Relation names are owner-visible and contestable.

### 6.5 Candidate generation

The system may propose a Muse only when grounded in:

- an explicit record mention;
- a source link;
- an owner prompt;
- a prior declared relation.

The system must not infer hidden emotional attachments from behavior alone.

Candidate generation never creates acknowledged standing.

### 6.6 Constellation topology

A Constellation is not a ranking.

Do not compute or display:

- “most important person”;
- influence leaderboard;
- emotional score;
- centrality as value;
- psychological diagnosis.

Permitted structural properties include:

- first appearance;
- recurrence;
- relation type;
- source link;
- event attachment;
- continuity across successors;
- visibility state.

### 6.7 Rendering law

Muses remain outside committed Core standing.

They may:

- orbit;
- recur;
- form constellations;
- connect to events;
- connect to other Muses;
- point to prior Pinecœnes;
- remain faint or private.

They may not:

- fill the Core;
- increase charge;
- masquerade as receipts;
- close an OPEN relation;
- acquire owner identity without permission.

Candidate and rejected Muses are Studio-only review material. They are excluded from the canonical Constellation, the admitted semantic digest, recipient packages, topology seeds, visual rendering, and sound rendering.

### 6.8 Constellation disclosure

The owner may independently choose:

- whether the Constellation is visible;
- which Muse identities are named;
- which are aliased;
- which remain abstract;
- which relations open;
- whether linked artifacts may be followed.

### 7.3 Address states

```text

```

### 7.4 Address law

Address does not create permission.

A confirmed Address may exist while the Locket remains closed.

An Offering must bind Address and disclosure separately.

### 7.5 Address privacy

Recipient identity is private by default.

A public Glyph must not expose the Address unless the owner explicitly enables it.

### 7.6 Multiple Addresses

Each Offering binds one declared Address context.

A single Fold may therefore be presented differently to:

- a loved one;
- a collaborator;
- a public audience;
- a future self.

The Fold remains immutable.

## 8 · Animation and Expression Profile

### 8.1 Definition

**Animation** is the human act of giving the compiled Pinecœne mood, dedication, sensory character, and presentation.

The technical object is an **Expression Profile**.

Animation does not mean merely adding motion. It uses the older sense of giving life or breath to a body.

### 8.2 Allowed controls

The owner may control:

- semantic-role hue mapping;
- particle tint and sparkle family;
- background or stage;
- reflected-light atmosphere;
- material finish;
- motion temperament;
- transition pacing within semantic timing bounds;
- sonic timbre;
- instrument family;
- spatialization;
- cover crop;
- frame;
- title;
- inscription;
- dedication;
- accessibility alternatives.

### 8.3 Protected architecture

The Expression Profile may not:

- change semantic topology;
- add or remove Core vertices;
- fabricate Quarta;
- convert candidate to admitted;
- hide meaningful gaps;
- alter closure state;
- increase charge;
- change source or reading;
- remove provenance;
- turn a Muse into a receipt;
- turn an Address into permission;
- change Locket resolution;
- mutate a sealed Fold.

### 8.4 Semantic-role color law

The owner may change hues.

The renderer must preserve distinction among:

- Solid;
- Return particulate;
- Muse particulate;
- Edge;
- OPEN;
- Charge.

Hue may vary. Semantic contrast may not collapse.

### 8.5 Motion temperament

Version 0.5 may support bounded presets:

```text
still
calm
tender
playful
solemn
bright
restless
ceremonial
```

A preset changes performance parameters, not event meaning.

No preset may make unresolved material appear resolved.

### 8.6 Sonic expression

The owner may choose timbre and instrumentation.

The event-to-sound relation remains fixed by the Sonic Profile.

A return remains a return.  
A gap remains a withheld event.  
Quarta remains a relational change rather than a louder sound.

### 8.7 Multiple Expression Profiles

A Fold may have many Expression Profiles.

Each profile is versioned and hashable.

Changing Expression creates a new presentation, not a successor Fold.

## 9 · Offering Canvas

### 9.1 Purpose

The Offering Canvas is the owner surface where a lawful Fold becomes a relational offering.

### 9.2 Canvas stages

#### Stage 1 — Recognize

- review candidate Muses;
- confirm, reject, alias, or hide;
- link prior Pinecœnes or source artifacts;
- inspect formative lineage.

#### Stage 2 — Animate

- choose palette;
- choose particle treatment;
- choose stage and light;
- choose motion temperament;
- choose sonic instrumentation;
- add title or dedication.

#### Stage 3 — Address

- select recipient or audience;
- write optional address text;
- confirm recipient privacy;
- choose whether Address is visible.

#### Stage 4 — Open

- choose disclosure resolution;
- choose Constellation visibility;
- choose source and Trail visibility;
- choose sound and replay permissions;
- choose download and persistence behavior.

#### Stage 5 — Preview

Preview exactly what the recipient will receive.

The preview must include:

- visual;
- sound;
- reduced-motion;
- sound-off;
- protected-content check;
- Address disclosure check.

#### Stage 6 — Offer

Create the Offering package or capability.

### 9.3 Canvas law

The Canvas may animate the body.

It may not edit the earned architecture.

Any request to change semantic topology routes back to Fold Studio and creates a candidate successor workflow.

### 9.4 Progressive authoring law

The Offering Canvas may expose the full grammar gradually. The first thin slice should organize the owner’s work around five human moments:

1. **What happened?** — select or create the authorized record.
2. **What shaped this?** — review and acknowledge Muses.
3. **How should it feel?** — animate the lawful body.
4. **Who is this for?** — confirm Address and disclosure.
5. **What may they receive?** — preview and offer.

Canonical terms remain visible in inspectable detail, documentation, and advanced controls. Progressive disclosure may simplify language; it may not collapse distinctions or hide authority changes.

The Preview stage must use the same Witness Player and the exact serialized disclosure projection that the recipient will receive. A separate imitation preview is non-conformant.

## 10 · Sharing and consent architecture

### 10.1 Resolutions

#### Resolution 0 — Glyph

Closed projection only.

#### Resolution 1 — Card

Glyph plus owner-selected metadata, title, and dedication.

#### Resolution 2 — Form

Full geometry and sound without protected proposition or receipt content.

#### Resolution 3 — Inspect

Selected source, reading, Muse, boundary, and return details.

#### Resolution 4 — Replay

Permitted Trail and becoming.

#### Resolution 5 — Recital

Ephemeral performed encounter with no compulsory persistent copy.

Resolution is a maximum disclosure ceiling, not a grant that overrides narrower permissions. Effective disclosure is the intersection of source visibility, owner standing, Muse visibility, Address visibility, resolution, Offering permissions, consent constraints, and recipient accessibility needs. When rules conflict, the most restrictive lawful rule wins and the Preview must explain the narrowing.

### 10.2 Offering modes

```text
glyph
locket
open_hands
recital
```

### 10.3 Offering properties

An Offering binds:

- Pinecœne ID;
- Fold version;
- Expression Profile ID;
- Address ID;
- resolution;
- Muse visibility;
- Trail visibility;
- sound permission;
- replay permission;
- download policy;
- expiry;
- revocation status.

### 10.4 Decline law

A recipient may decline without penalty.

Decline does not:

- lower owner standing;
- create a negative Muse relation;
- create a failure mark;
- imply rejection of the owner;
- become public.

### 10.5 Locket law

Recognition never creates entitlement.

Seeing a Glyph or Locket never grants Form, Inspect, Replay, or Recital access.

### 10.6 Carrier law

7 or another carrier may present the Offering.

The carrier may not:

- open protected resolution;
- inherit the Fold;
- inherit charge;
- alter Address;
- alter Expression;
- accept on behalf of the recipient.

### 10.7 Shared-material consent

Before an Offering is compiled, the owner must declare whether the authorized record contains material created with or about another living person.

The declaration must distinguish at minimum:

- owner-only material;
- shared conversation or co-created material;
- quoted or linked public material;
- private third-party material;
- material involving a child or protected person;
- memorial material involving a deceased person.

This declaration does not automatically establish legal permission. It determines which consent checks and disclosure warnings the product must present.

The owner must explicitly confirm the right to offer the selected projection. The system must support exclusion, aliasing, narrowing, and withdrawal of third-party material without rewriting the sealed predecessor.

A recipient controls whether their Return is:

- private to the encounter;
- available to the owner as candidate successor material;
- exportable or linkable;
- later retractable where transport permits.

Silence is never consent. Relationship, familiarity, or prior receipt of an Offering is never blanket permission for future Offerings.

### 10.8 Canonical origin and transport honesty

The web product must have one canonical origin because browser-local custody is origin-scoped.

The first production origin is:

```text
https://pinecoene.com
```

`https://www.pinecoene.com`, `https://pinecœne.com`, and their equivalent punycode or `www` forms must permanently redirect to the canonical origin before application state is written.

Direct and relay transport must state their limits plainly:

- relay revocation can prevent future retrieval but cannot revoke a package already downloaded;
- `allowDownload: false` can suppress an offered download control but cannot prevent copying of bytes already delivered to a recipient device;
- Recital can avoid creating a compulsory persistent copy but cannot prevent screenshots, screen recording, photography, or human recollection;
- a self-contained direct package is intentionally usable without the relay and therefore cannot depend on later relay revocation.

The interface must not claim stronger control than the carrier can enforce.

## 11 · Encounter, Return, and Successor

### 11.1 Encounter

A recipient may:

- rotate;
- listen;
- inspect permitted layers;
- replay permitted transitions;
- follow permitted Muse links;
- read the Address;
- respond;
- request deeper resolution;
- decline.

The recipient may not mutate the Fold.

### 11.2 Return kinds

```text
acknowledgment
question
reflection
objection
receipt
memory
muse
address_back
pinecoene
decline
silence
```

Silence is not automatically recorded as negative standing.

### 11.3 Return admission

A recipient Return arrives as candidate record material.

The owner may:

- admit;
- reject;
- narrow;
- leave OPEN;
- attach as Muse;
- attach as receipt;
- attach as Address;
- exclude from successor.

The system must not decide the semantic class solely from message content.

### 11.4 Successor law

A sealed Fold never mutates.

If admitted returns change the record:

```text
predecessor Fold
  + admitted return events
  + declared reading
  + owner seal
  → successor Fold
```

The predecessor remains replayable and addressable.

### 11.5 Docking

A successor may dock beside its predecessor.

A returned Pinecœne may dock without merging.

> **Shapes may touch. They do not collapse into one another by default.**

### 11.6 Encounter Overture

The default recipient encounter should feel like receiving a letter, not entering software.

Before opening, the recipient sees:

- who sent the Offering, when disclosure permits;
- the dedication or Address text;
- the closed Glyph or Locket;
- a concise statement of what opening reveals and what the sender may learn;
- three quiet choices: **Open**, **Not now**, and **Decline**.

No account is required before this value is available. Sound never autoplays. Opening begins only after a recipient action.

The first thirty seconds should follow this score:

1. the closed object rests under raking light without decorative idle motion;
2. the recipient chooses to open;
3. lawful Edges separate and Solids acquire depth;
4. Return particulate arrives as discrete, directional events;
5. acknowledged Muses awaken at the periphery, never inside committed Core standing;
6. Address becomes an orientation of the whole composition;
7. any OPEN relation remains an unjoined seam, missing face, or unresolved cadence;
8. the object settles into an inspectable state.

Selecting any meaningful feature reveals an explanation appropriate to the Offering resolution:

- what semantic class the feature represents;
- which event or admitted relation caused it;
- what source, reading, and standing are permitted to be shown;
- why the feature remains open, bounded, or sealed.

This is **Inspectable Causality**. It is the primary source of wonder and trust. Visual complexity without causal legibility is non-conformant.

## 14 · Compiler architecture

### 14.1 Fold compiler

```ts
compileFold(
  score: PinecoeneScore,
  compilerVersion: string
): SemanticConformation
```

The compiler must be pure and deterministic.

It must not depend on:

- DOM;
- Three.js;
- Canvas;
- Web Audio;
- React;
- wall-clock time;
- network state;
- unseeded randomness;
- UI state.

The canonical pipeline is:

```text
authorized record
  → validated score
  → admitted semantic digest
  → pure deterministic Fold compiler
  → immutable semantic conformation + transition score
  → disclosure compiler
  → recipient-safe Offering payload
  → visual / audio / semantic-text renderers
```

The browser interface, renderer, relay, and package carrier are consumers of this pipeline. None is the source of semantic truth.

### 14.2 Compiler outputs

#### Semantic topology

- Core;
- Bag;
- Return Field;
- Muse Constellation;
- Address candidate loci;
- OPEN relations;
- permissions.

#### Transition score

- appear;
- return;
- collide;
- divide;
- recur;
- acknowledge;
- admit;
- seal;
- address;
- offer;
- remain open.

#### Semantic-to-topology law

The first compiler profile must publish a versioned mapping table. At minimum:

| Semantic condition | Canonical topology effect | Motion event | Sonic event |
|---|---|---|---|
| admitted commitment | creates or strengthens Solid according to declared role | weight-bearing placement | stable harmonic body |
| admitted Return | creates Return particulate attached to its event locus | directional arrival, collision, or settling | discrete struck or plucked event |
| acknowledged Muse | creates peripheral Constellation topology only | recurrence or spatial appearance | recurring timbral or spatial motif |
| boundary or withheld relation | creates Edge, gate, membrane, or meaningful interval | interruption, measure, or refusal to cross | silence, gate, or bounded resonance |
| OPEN relation | preserves an unjoined seam, missing face, or unresolved path | no false settling | unresolved or continuing cadence |
| admitted Quarta | changes dimension, adjacency, or topology under an explicit admission rule | structural reorientation | new harmonic dimensionality |
| Address | orients the completed composition without opening it | whole-form posture or direction | spatial orientation only |

Every renderer feature must retain the originating semantic event ID or relation ID. A visual or sonic feature without such lineage is decorative expression and may not imply standing.

Quarta admission requires an explicit declared reading and owner admission event. Recurrence, brightness, model confidence, or visual opportunity cannot create Quarta.

### 14.3 Constellation compiler

The Constellation compiler accepts only the admitted Constellation stored in `PinecoeneScore`.

Candidate, rejected, and otherwise unadmitted Muse review objects remain outside the canonical score. Mention and linkage facts may qualify an acknowledged Muse; they do not replace owner acknowledgment.

Adding, removing, or reordering rejected candidates must not change:

- admitted semantic digest;
- score hash;
- topology seed;
- topology;
- Transition score;
- visual output;
- sonic output;
- disclosure projection.

### 14.4 Expression renderer

The Expression Profile is applied after semantic compilation.

```ts
perform(
  conformation: SemanticConformation,
  expression: ExpressionProfile,
  rendererProfile: RendererProfile
): RenderablePerformance
```

The renderer validates that Expression does not alter protected architecture.

### 14.5 Offering compiler

```ts
compileOffering(
  fold: SemanticConformation,
  expression: ExpressionProfile,
  address: Address,
  permissions: OfferingPermissions
): OfferingPackage
```

Before packaging, a pure disclosure compiler must project the Fold, Expression, Address snapshot, and permission envelope into a recipient-safe object.

```ts
compileDisclosure(
  fold: SemanticConformation,
  expression: ExpressionProfile,
  address: Address,
  permissions: OfferingPermissions
): DisclosureProjection
```

Protected fields are absent from `DisclosureProjection`; they are not shipped and hidden with CSS, component state, shader uniforms, unreachable controls, or client-side permission checks.

The Offering compiler hashes and serializes the disclosure projection actually received by the Witness Player. Preview, direct package, and relay retrieval must use byte-equivalent authorized projections for the same Offering.

### 14.6 Successor compiler

A successor requires:

- predecessor reference;
- new admitted record events;
- declared reading;
- owner admission;
- new score hash;
- new Pinecœne ID or version according to identity law.

### 14.7 Determinism profile

Determinism applies to semantic output, not guaranteed identical pixels across all devices.

Each compiler version must declare:

- the exact pseudo-random algorithm, not only its seed;
- numeric quantization rules for layout instructions;
- stable ordering and tie-breaking rules;
- canonical string and Unicode handling;
- semantic snapshot fixtures;
- compatibility rules for prior compiler versions.

The renderer may adapt pixel density, antialiasing, and non-semantic quality to device capability. It may not change topology, semantic event order, disclosure, standing, OPEN state, or the presence of admitted features.

## 15 · Render profiles

### 15.1 Visual profile

Normative:

- Solid remains committed matter;
- Return particulate and Muse particulate remain distinguishable;
- Edge remains boundary and relation;
- Quarta produces a genuine dimensional or topological change rather than a flash, flourish, or reward effect;
- Address is orientation, not permission;
- OPEN remains visible;
- charge remains computed;
- Expression cannot hide semantic state;
- a sealed predecessor remains fixed;
- a successor is visibly related but distinct.

### 15.2 Constellation visual behavior

Recommended default:

- Return Field inside or near the Bag;
- Muse Constellation outside or around the Return Field;
- linked Muses may connect to prior Pinecœnes;
- private Muses render abstract or remain hidden;
- recurring Muses may reappear without becoming brighter by default;
- Address points outward from the Fold toward the offering context.

The exact composition remains renderer freedom.

### 15.3 Sonic profile

Normative:

- Solid → stable harmonic body;
- Return particulate → discrete returned events;
- Muse particulate → recurring motifs, timbral references, or spatial presences;
- Edge → measure, gate, silence, relation;
- Address → directional or relational orientation;
- Quarta → new harmonic dimensionality;
- OPEN → unresolved or continuing state;
- Expression may change instrument, not event class.

### 15.4 Glyph profile

The Glyph may encode:

- topology;
- substance distribution;
- openness;
- Constellation presence at abstract level;
- version.

It must not leak:

- Muse identity;
- recipient identity;
- protected source;
- private Address;
- receipt content.

### 15.5 Accessibility profile

Provide:

- keyboard traversal;
- screen-reader topology;
- source/read/standing labels;
- Muse versus receipt distinction;
- Address description;
- reduced-motion mode;
- sound-off mode;
- nonvisual Offering preview;
- high-contrast semantic separation.

Accessibility preferences override Expression settings. An owner cannot require motion, sound, low contrast, precise pointer control, or WebGL as a condition of understanding the permitted Offering.

### 15.6 First production art direction

The canonical first production renderer should present the Pinecœne as a tactile folded object in a dark, quiet field with editorial typography and restrained physical light.

Preferred qualities:

- raking light that reveals edges, depth, and material truth;
- paper, mineral, glass, metal, and soft finishes with physically credible response;
- shadow as structure, not decoration;
- sparse color assigned by semantic role;
- deliberate negative space;
- event-scored movement with full periods of rest;
- close, material sound: fold, strike, scrape, breath, resonance, and silence;
- typography that feels literary and archival rather than technological.

Avoid:

- bloom, lens flare, chromatic aberration, and excessive depth of field as a substitute for form;
- neon cyberpunk palettes;
- galaxy fields and ambient particle fog;
- hover-only meaning;
- scroll-jacking;
- autoplay sound;
- synthetic voiceover in the recipient overture;
- continuous idle motion for a sealed Fold;
- interface chrome competing with the Offered Object.

### 15.7 Fallback and stillness profiles

Every Offering must provide:

- a meaningful static first frame or canonical 2D Glyph;
- a reduced-motion performance that preserves event order and causality;
- a non-WebGL semantic-text and image path;
- captions or text alternatives for sonic events;
- keyboard and touch access to Inspectable Causality;
- a sound-off experience that remains complete.

The fallback is not an error screen. It is a lawful renderer of the same disclosure projection.

### 16.9 Integrity

```text
canonicalScore       = JCS(hashableScorePayload(validatedScore))
scoreHash            = SHA-256(frame("score", schemaVersion, canonicalScore))
semanticDigest       = JCS(admittedSemanticProjection(validatedScore))
semanticHash         = SHA-256(frame("semantic", schemaVersion, semanticDigest))
topologySeed         = SHA-256(frame("topology", semanticHash, compilerVersion, prngVersion))
expressionHash       = SHA-256(frame("expression", JCS(expressionPayload)))
disclosureHash       = SHA-256(frame("disclosure", JCS(disclosureProjection)))
offeringHash         = SHA-256(frame("offering", JCS(offeringPayload)))
```

`frame` is a specified length-prefixed or structurally canonical encoding. Raw string concatenation is non-conformant.

`hashableScorePayload` includes the authorized score, admitted Constellation, permission envelope, consent declaration, compiler declaration, and predecessor reference, while excluding candidate review material and derived hash fields. `admittedSemanticProjection` contains only topology-bearing admitted meaning. Changing consent, disclosure, or transport permissions may change `scoreHash`; it must not cosmetically rearrange the same admitted semantic form.

Hash fields are excluded from their own inputs. JCS canonicalization does not imply Unicode normalization; source strings are preserved byte-for-byte, and any normalization of derived labels must be declared and versioned.

SHA-256 establishes content integrity, not human identity, authorship, or consent. Any later signature system is a separate profile.
